How NELDO handles your information

Version 5 · Effective November 1, 2026

NELDO is for adults 18 and older in the United States, on iPhone and Android.

A short, plain guide. The Privacy Notice and Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy have the full details and your rights.

The short version

  • No account. Your chats, saved meals, lists and kitchen stay on your phone.
  • To answer you, NELDO sends what it needs to NELDO's servers and to outside AI companies. They don't use it to train their AI, and NELDO's servers don't store your messages or photos.
  • New: the recipe from a meal NELDO makes for you can go back into NELDO's recipe library, rewritten in NELDO's own words. Never your words or anything about you. Turn off "Share new recipes" in Settings → Privacy & AI to stop it.
  • Questions? Email support@neldo.app, or use "Ask NELDO about our rules" in the app.

Do I need an account or my name?

No. NELDO has no accounts. A name is optional; if you add one, it stays on your phone and goes along with chat requests so NELDO can greet you. Please remember that anything you type or show, like a name or a place, can identify you, and that the app uses a random ID to keep your requests working together.

What stays on my phone?

Your conversation (the last 60 messages), saved meals and favorites, weekly plans, grocery and kitchen lists, settings, and permission choices. NELDO doesn't sync them to a server. On Android, NELDO turns off app-data backup; on iPhone, your own device backup may include them.

What leaves my phone, and who gets it?

Only after you tap Accept in the app. If you used NELDO before this version, the updated app shows a short screen once and asks you to accept again before new AI requests are sent. NELDO needs AI to work. When you ask for something, the app sends your message, your recent conversation, relevant settings (like your name, how you eat, goals, and foods you never want), kitchen items, and the recipe you're on to NELDO's server, which passes it to an outside AI company to write the answer. On some messages, a second outside AI company also checks it for safety at the same time. If one of them is down or fails, other outside AI companies are the backups. NELDO's server doesn't save your messages. It keeps only security and cost records without content, and deletes them on a schedule (see the retention table). The outside AI companies keep requests for a limited time, up to 30 days at our main one, mainly to watch for abuse. None of them is allowed to use them to improve its AI. Want to know which outside AI companies NELDO uses? Email support@neldo.app and we'll send you the list. Crash reports leave your phone only if you turn on "Send crash reports" in Settings → Privacy & AI. It starts off and works only while AI help is on. A report goes to Google's Firebase Crashlytics with the error, app and phone details, and a random install ID, never your chats, and Google keeps it about 90 days.

What happens to a pantry photo?

Photo scanning is in testing. You take or pick up to 3 photos. Each one goes, by itself, to NELDO's server and then to an outside AI company (or another one as backup), which lists the food it sees. Only that ingredient list comes back. NELDO's server holds the photo only in memory for your request and doesn't save it. The outside AI company may keep it for a time under its terms. Your original stays in your photo library. NELDO's server removes hidden photo details, like where a photo was taken and which camera took it, before the AI sees it. Keep people and documents out of the shot, or type your ingredients instead.

Does NELDO train AI with my content?

No. NELDO doesn't use your content to train, fine-tune, or distill AI models, and it doesn't sell your data. It has no ads today, and if that ever changes, ads will be clearly labeled, never in your chats or meals and never based on your personal data. Under our agreements with them, the outside AI companies aren't allowed to use NELDO's requests to train their AI either. Recipes from meals made for you (never your words or details about you) can be added to NELDO's recipe library, which is a recipe book, not an AI model (see below).

Can NELDO make medical decisions?

No. NELDO is for everyday planning and general wellness. It can leave out foods you never want or follow a limit you give it, including one from your doctor, but it can't check food allergies or cross-contact, so check every ingredient and label yourself. It doesn't diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent conditions or change medication. Nutrition numbers are estimates. If a message suggests someone may be in danger, NELDO shows 988 and 911. Common crisis phrases are caught on your phone itself, even without a connection. Your phone also shows 911 right away, without asking AI, when a message describes an overdose, a severe allergic reaction, a heart attack or a stroke happening now. That message isn't sent at that moment. It stays in your chat on your phone, so if you keep chatting it can go along as recent conversation. "Start fresh" clears it. The 911 answer works even with AI help off or no connection, but it can miss a message. In an emergency, call 911 first.

How do I delete things or stop?

In the app: "Start fresh" clears the conversation, "Clear my NELDO data" erases everything NELDO keeps on your phone, and Settings → Privacy & AI lets you turn AI help off (NELDO needs AI to work, so chat and new meals stop while it's off) or turn off "Send crash reports." Turn off "Share new recipes" there to stop new recipes from your meals joining the recipe library; a recipe already in the library has no link to you, so it can't be found and removed. You can also remove camera or photo access in your phone's settings. For anything else, like a safety report (keep its case ID), email support@neldo.app. Requests already sent to outside AI companies can't be pulled back.

What is the recipe library?

NELDO keeps a library of recipes made by NELDO. Many meals you see come from it, for speed and low cost. NELDO writes these recipes with AI, starting from a simple outline, like a Mexican dinner for a low-energy night, or from the recipe of a meal NELDO made for someone (below). The words people type to NELDO never go into them. Every recipe goes through NELDO's recipe and food-safety checks before it's used. The checks are automatic. The recipes are in English, Spanish and Portuguese.

When a library recipe fits what you asked for, NELDO can use it instead of writing a new one. That's faster, and it helps keep NELDO free. NELDO's AI still talks with you. If you ask for something the library can't fully match, like cooking with what's in your fridge, a dish with specific ingredients, a change to a meal, or a limit from your doctor, the AI writes a meal just for you.

What's never in the library: your messages, your name, your photos, your settings, your kitchen list, anything about your health, or anything else that points to you.

New: recipes from meals made for you. The recipe from a meal NELDO's AI makes for you (ingredients, steps, times, and recipe details like cuisine, servings, kitchen equipment and how much effort it takes, rewritten by NELDO in its own words with its own title) can go back into the library, to help everyone get better meals. This starts only after you accept this version in the app, and only while "Share new recipes" is on. NELDO never keeps your words, chat, notes, name, photos, kitchen list, location, diet or Never include list, or your app or session ID, and it keeps only the week a meal was made, not the time. It skips meals from crisis, distress or medical chats, typed allergy words, nutrition goals, a health professional's limits, baby food, cooking help with your own pantry, meals made by the backup AI, and meals you reported or gave a thumbs down. A recipe waits at least 7 days, gets NELDO's own details (the recipe in English, Spanish and Portuguese, spice level, leftovers, make-ahead and a cost range) from one small AI call that holds only the recipe, and passes every library check before anyone else gets it. Once it's in the library, nothing links it to you, so it can't be traced and removed. Turn off "Share new recipes" in Settings → Privacy & AI any time to stop new additions. The Privacy Notice, section 15.1 has the details.

What NELDO counts: how often each recipe is served, and which kinds of meals the library doesn't have yet (by cuisine, meal and energy, each month). These are plain totals. They aren't linked to you or your phone.

Votes: when you rate a library recipe, the app sends only the recipe and your vote, and NELDO adds one to that recipe's total. Your phone remembers which recipes you rated, so you rate each one once. If you report a library recipe as wrong or dislike it, NELDO's server also keeps a short one-way tag made from part of your network address, never the address itself, with that recipe's totals. A dislike also keeps one with the totals for that kind of meal. A recipe's tags are cleared when NELDO reviews the recipe. These tags only help NELDO tell whether votes and reports come from different places, and they can't point to you. Recipes people don't like, or report as wrong, come out of rotation for review. They aren't deleted.

Leaving foods out: if a recipe has something you never include, NELDO drops it if it's optional, uses its no-pork or vegetarian version, picks another recipe, or has the AI write one without it. It always tells you what changed. Check labels on store-bought items.

Allergies: the library has no allergy information. NELDO can't check for allergies or cross-contact.

Training: the library is never used to train AI. Neither is anything you type.

Can I ask about these rules?

Yes. Email support@neldo.app, or use "Ask NELDO about our rules" in the app (Settings → Privacy & AI). It answers only from the Privacy Notice and the Terms, and it can explain what the words mean. It is not a lawyer and gives no legal advice. NELDO doesn't store your questions. Common questions are answered right on your phone. If yours needs more and AI help is on, it may go to an outside AI company only to find the right sentences.
